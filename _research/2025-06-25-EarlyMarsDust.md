This project investigated how the optical properties of airborne and surface
dust influence the climate of early Mars. The results indicate that basaltic
dust representative of reducing conditions produces net cooling over
physically plausible optical depths, and that dust radiative effects alone
are insufficient to resolve the faint young Sun paradox.

The project also quantified uncertainties in surface albedo arising from
particle size, mineral composition, and particle-mixing geometry.
